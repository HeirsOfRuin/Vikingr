## A. Character creation
- **Attributes, point-buy.**
  - The pool is the beginning's total (11, as now), spread across strength, agility, endurance, wits and presence.
  - Each attribute is 1–5.
  - The screen shows what each one does, in a line.
  - The beginning's spread is the starting point, so tapping Begin without changing anything plays as before.
- **Three skill sets per beginning**, 7 points each, as now. The current set is the first.
  - **The younger son:** raised to the sword (as now); raised to the steering-oar (seamanship, command); raised at the jarl's table (speech, lore, cunning).
  - **The outlaw:** the killer (as now); the poacher (cunning, seamanship, weapons); the talker (speech, cunning, weapons).
  - **The freedman's son:** the boatwright (as now); the trader's boy (trade, speech, seamanship); the Irish grandmother's grandson (lore, speech, cunning).
- **Saves and bots** are unchanged: the defaults are today's numbers.

## B. Family life, and the heir
**Children.**
- A married hero has a child about every other winter, son or daughter, named for kin, and renamable on the Home tab.
- Some die young (about one in four before five years old), as they did. Each birth and each death has a short scene.
- The Home tab gets a Family card: names, ages, where each one is.

**What a child can do:**
- **A son of 7 to 14** can be fostered with an ally (a lord or a captain who thinks well of you). It binds them: favour or regard rises each year. The son comes home at 14 knowing what that hall knows.
- **A son of 14 or more** can sail with you as a sworn man. He is loyal to the bone, and can die.
- **A daughter of 15 or more** can be married into an alliance: a captain's son, a lord's house. It's a large and lasting regard, and her bride-price comes to you.

**The heir.**
- When you die and a son lives, the death screen offers to **go on as him**.
- **The years pass** until he is 16. The chronicle runs year by year on the same engine: the legends raid, kings fall, ports change hands.
- **"The years between"** summary then tells, plainly:
  - what the Vikings did while he grew: the news he heard by the fire, year by year;
  - what happened at home: his mother kept the farm; some of the hoard was spent; the old men drifted off; the ships rotted or were sold;
  - what he inherits.
- **He inherits:**
  - the farm or hall;
  - the hoard (less a share a year);
  - his father's lasting fame as "son of X" (half of it, told at once);
  - his father's honour and feuds (the ports remember);
  - the old regard of captains and lords (halved);
  - bought land (granted land lapses with the oath; a fort is lost unless his father's men held it).
- **He starts at 16** at the spring muster, with his own character creation (point-buy and skill set). A short scene stands him at his father's mound.
- **A son already grown** (16+) takes over at once, with no time skip.

## C. Settlement
**The move.**
- **Where:** at any holding you own (a ship-fort, or land you bought or were granted), in winter or with your ships there.
- **What it takes:** "Bring your family and your men's families, and settle here". It needs room in the ships for the families (hold space) and a summer's food. The hall at home is sold or left to kin.
- **What moves:** the wife, the children, the thralls and the hoard.

**From then on, the holding is home:**
- you winter there as at home, with a full winter, feasts and the yield;
- you muster there, from the settlers' sons and the land (a muster pool of its own);
- the share-out and the end of the summer happen there;
- "Sail home" in your orders goes there.

**The men's families settle too.**
- The crew's spirits rise.
- The sworn men's loyalty rises; their wives are here.
- No sworn man leaves "for home" at the turn of the year.

**What stays behind:**
- **Scenes written for Hordaland stay in Hordaland.** The jarl's levy, the district's feasts and the old neighbours don't follow you; that is what leaving means.
- **A ship-fort home can still be attacked** in the winter, and you will be there to fight for it.

**Iceland's landnám** (E) is the same move, to land nobody holds.

## D. Ragnarr's days (843)
- **The era is chosen at a new game:** "Horik's days, 830" or "Ragnarr's days, 843". Each beginning works in either.
- **In 843 the world has moved on.** The chronicle from 830 to 843 is applied before the first scene: Dorestad stripped, ship-forts at Dublin and Linn Duachaill, Turgeis at his height. The jarl in Hordaland is Eiríkr Eysteinsson.
- **An opening chapter** at the spring muster of 843: the Vestfold war-arrow for Nantes, rowing at another man's oar, as in 830.
  - The Loire, St John's Day, and a winter on Noirmoutier with the young Hastein.
  - In 844, news of Seville, and a chance at a ship of your own.
  - In 845, Ragnarr's arrow for Paris (the existing chapter), and Turgeis's fall in Ireland.
  - About 6 new scenes. The rest is the existing world.
- **The bots** play 843 lives in the measurements, so the era's balance is measured, not guessed.

## E. Iceland
- **The map grows west** to take Iceland in (the grid is regenerated from Natural Earth; places keep their longitude and latitude).
- **Places:**
  - **Snæland/Garðarshólmr**, found in the 860s: rumoured from the Faroes, reached by expedition. Before then, only Irish hermits.
  - **Ingólfr's settlement** at Reykjarvík, from 870.
  - **The Breiðafjörðr coast** for land-taking.
- **A port:** trade in walrus ivory, wool, falcons. No raiding: there is nobody to raid.
- **Landnám:** claim land in Iceland, which becomes a holding with no price but the voyage and the winters. Then settle (C): end your saga as a founder.
- **A short chapter:** first sight of land, the high-seat pillars thrown overboard, the first winter.
- **Dates follow the Landnámabók tradition.** The text says so.

## What will be measured
- **A:** bot lives with each skill set: no set dominant on survival or fame.
- **B:** births per married life, children alive at the father's death, and how many lives go on as an heir. Measured over 40-year family runs.
- **C:** settled lives (losses to fort attacks, muster size against the home fjord).
- **D:** 843 lives against 830 lives (survival, fame, plunder).
- **E:** Iceland reached and settled, per life that tries.

## Built

### A. Character creation
- **Choose the era, the name and the beginning, then a second screen:**
  - **What you were born with:** an 11-point pool across the five attributes, each 1–4 (not the framed 1–5; see the measurements), with + and − buttons. Each attribute has one line saying what it does in the rules.
  - **How you were raised:** three skill sets per beginning, 7 points each; the skills show under each one.
  - **Begin** stays greyed out until every point is spent. Left untouched, it is the beginning as before, so old saves and the bots are unchanged.
- **Skill sets:**
  - **The younger son:** the sword / the steering-oar / the jarl's table.
  - **The outlaw:** the killer / the poacher / the talker.
  - **The freedman's son:** the boatwright / the trader's boy / the Irish grandmother's grandson.
- The same screen is used for the heir.

### B. Family and the heir
- **Births:** a married man who winters at home has a child with a 1 in 2 chance a winter (1 in 7 the winter after a birth).
  - The first son and the first daughter have their own scenes (the existing ones, which now name the child).
  - Later children share a birth scene, where you name the child.
- **Deaths:** about 1 in 4 children die before five, each with a short scene.
- **The Home tab's Family card:** names, ages, where each child is, and Rename.
- **What a child can do:**
  - **A son of 7–13 is fostered** with a lord or captain who thinks well of you. It binds them at once (+2) and each year after (+1). He comes home at 14.
  - **A son of 14 or more sails as a sworn man,** loyalty pinned at 10. If he dies at the oar, the child dies with him.
  - **A daughter of 15 or more marries into an ally's house:** +4 regard, and a bride-price of 1 pound from a captain's house or 2 pounds from a lord's.
- **The heir:**
  - **The offer:** the death screen offers to go on as a living son, eldest first, or as a brother if there is no son.
  - **The years pass** until he is 16, or to the next spring if he is already grown. The chronicle runs year by year on the same engine.
  - **"The years between"** lists up to three pieces of news a year, the annals first. It then says what happened at home:
    - the hoard less 15% a year;
    - each old sworn man has a 1 in 4 chance a year of going;
    - the ships other than the best are sold;
    - the last ship rots after ten winters.
  - **He inherits:**
    - the farm or hall;
    - what is left of the hoard;
    - half his father's fame, as one deed: "Son of X";
    - his father's honour and every port's memory of him;
    - half the regard of captains and lords, and half the hamingja;
    - bought land.
  - **He loses:**
    - granted land and the oath;
    - a fort, unless the son was at the oar or an old sworn man stayed, and at most a winter passed;
    - the high seat;
    - outlawry, courting and betrothals (they were his father's).
  - **Then:**
    - the heir's own point-buy and upbringing;
    - a scene at his father's mound;
    - the spring muster.
  - **His upbringing:** two sets are always offered (the farm, his father's old men). The third depends on his years: at his father's oar, fostered in a lord's hall, fostered aboard a captain's ship, or raised on the old men's stories.
- **The chronicle now runs to 878:**
  - 23 dated events, from London and Thanet (851) through Horik's death, the Seine Danes and Björn and Hastein's southern voyage, to the Great Army, York, Edmund, Dumbarton, Alfred, Hafrsfjord and Edington, plus the Iceland news;
  - three new raiding legends: the Seine Danes, Olaf in Ireland, and the Great Army.

### C. Settlement
- **At any land you hold abroad:** "Bring your family and your men's families, and settle here."
  - **Souls to carry:** the household, the thralls, 2 for each sworn man, and 1 for every 3 oarsmen.
  - **Room:** 4 souls for each point of a ship's cargo. A knarr carries 40, so most settlers need one.
  - **Food:** 30 days for each soul.
  - **The old farm or hall** is sold for half its price; a man with no farm pays half a farm's price to raise a longhouse.
  - The move takes 24 days.
- **From then on, the holding is home:**
  - the spring muster, the winter as at home, births, the hall fire, the share-out;
  - "sail home" in coast orders and passage home.
- **The muster there:** the settlers' sons (4) and the land's men, in place of the jarl's favour.
- **The men:** morale +2 at once, and the crew's morale tends to 7 instead of 6. Sworn men gain +2 loyalty, and their loyalty never falls below 3, so none walks.
- **A ship-fort that is home:**
  - **If you winter there,** they come for it at the usual odds, and you fight it as a battle at the rampart against 1.4 times the place's defenders. You can also put the families in the ships and give it up.
  - **If you are away,** it can fall without you. The household gets away to Hordaland, with half the hoard lost and the hall burned.
- **What stays behind:** the Hordaland scenes (they require the home fjord) stay there.

### D. Ragnarr's days
- **The new-game screen** offers "Horik's days, 830" or "Ragnarr's days, 843". All three beginnings work in either.
- **843 runs the chronicle from 830 to 842 first:**
  - places are sacked and mend;
  - Dublin and Linn Duachaill become ship-forts;
  - Eiríkr is jarl;
  - the 830–835 chapters are past.
- **A "Spring, 843" card** lists one piece of news a year, as the boy heard it.
- **The opening chapter** (5 scenes):
  1. **The boyhood:** what you did with thirteen winters of news (two skill points).
  2. **The Vestfold arrow:** a bench in Ölvir's Hart. The fleet sails at midsummer for the Loire, and the existing Nantes scenes take it from there.
  3. **The winter on Noirmoutier:** the salt-pans, the Breton river guides, or the young captains' talk. Hastein's own scene can fire there too.
  4. **Spring 844:** the fleet splits for Spain, and Ölvir sells you the Hart, for half down or all on your word.
  5. **Spring 845:** Ölvir comes for his silver. Paying it is +1 honour; asking for another year costs honour and the jarl's favour.
- **The outlaw** starts at Kormt, outside the district's law. His exile ends in 846, with a scene.
- **The 830 outlaw scenes** are shut in 843.

### E. Iceland
- **The map grows west** to 25°W and north to 67°N, at the same tile size (552×368).
  - Iceland's highlands and four glaciers are drawn in.
  - Islands smaller than a tile that the story needs (Iona, Noirmoutier, Lindisfarne, Rathlin, Skellig) are forced to land.
- **Two places, Ísland (Reykjarvík) and Breiðafjörðr:**
  - **Heard of from 860:** the Faroes' old sailors tell of it, and it is reached by expedition from the Faroes.
  - **Land is free to take from 870:**
    - carry fire round its bounds, a seamanship and endurance check;
    - or, with a hall, throw the high-seat pillars overboard.
    - The land yields 12 a year and 2 men.
  - **Reykjarvík trades from 874** (Ingólfr): walrus ivory, wool, eider down and gyrfalcons, a new good that sells in the English and Frankish towns.
- **Scenes:** first sight of land (it reads differently before 870, 870–873 and after 874), the land-taking, and the first winter (a craft and wits check).
- **Settling in Iceland** gives a founder's deed of weight 12. It counts as a verse, so it lasts.
- **The chronicle tells** of Garðarr (861), Flóki (868) and Ingólfr (874). The text and the notes say these are the Landnámabók's tradition, written some 250 years later.
