# The Year 831: Frame

**Status: built** (2026-10-12), `content/scenes/831/`. Waiting on the author's playtest. Built on the author's instruction ("expand into the next year and expand the map with a further 4 locations", 2026-10-12). The recommendations below were taken as decided so the build could go straight ahead. Any of them can be changed after the playtest.

**The year in one line:** the boy who went west on another man's bench now has a name, perhaps a ship, and a district watching him. 831 asks what he does with it: trade, raid further, settle, or take a king.

---

## 1. The four new places

| Place | Where | Kind | What it is for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kaupang** (Skíringssalr) | Vík, east of home | market | **Trade and gear.** Sell captives at a better price, buy a mail shirt or a Frankish sword, and hear news of the Danish king Horik. |
| **Lindisfarne** | Northumbria | monastery | **A new raid route,** east across the open sea. A tidal island: the causeway and the tide shape the raid. The gospel book with the jewelled cover. |
| **Skye** (Skíð) | The Hebrides | hall | **Settling, not raiding.** A Norse settler's steading in a sea loch. Men to hire, and an offer of land and a wife: the first glimpse of the settler career (DESIGN §21a). Irish hermits (papar) on an islet. |
| **Conaille** (Dún Delgan) | Dundalk Bay, Ireland | hall | **The king.** The Annals of Ulster for 831 record the first plundering of Conaille, and its king Máel Brigte and his brother Canannán carried off to the ships. The hero can be there: take the king, ransom him, or kill him, and drink from his cup (the Yule boast). |

**Sea roads:**
- Kaupang: from home and from Avaldsnes.
- Lindisfarne: from Orkney down the coast, or straight across the open sea from home (faster and riskier).
- Skye: from Orkney and from Iona.
- Conaille: from Bangor.

---

## 2. The beats

1. **Spring council** (all paths). Jarl Eystein's hall hears the news of the world, and the four places become known.
   - **A captain** can take the jarl's commission (carry his goods to Kaupang) or promise him the king of Conaille.
   - **An oarsman** hears his captain's plan: Orkney, Skye, Conaille. He can argue for Lindisfarne instead.
2. **Gyða's price** (if courting). Bárð names the bride-price, which falls due at the winter nights.
3. **The summer.** Captains sail free; oarsmen follow the route.
   - Authored scenes at all four new places.
   - Return visits to Birsay (Ingibjörg and her red thread) and Iona (Colmán, if he lived).
   - Four new sea events, written in both an oarsman's and a captain's voice.
4. **The homecoming.**
5. **Winter 831:**
   - Gyða's wedding, or the price unpaid and her father's other choice.
   - Hallbjörn and the boundary stone, if the brother feud is fated and provoked.
   - The Hrappsson brothers, if he refused them.
   - Yule, where the boasts are reckoned.
6. **Spring 832.** The written story pauses. The oarsman gets a second chance at a ship. The sandbox carries on.

## 3. Engine additions
- **Effects:**
  - `sell_captives` (a market price), for Kaupang;
  - `recruit` (men signed away from home), for Skye.
- **Gear:**
  - a mail shirt (`flag.mail`) raises his armour;
  - a bought Frankish sword (`flag.frankish_sword`) hits like his grandfather's.
- **Condition path:** `winter` (the year a winter belongs to), so winter scenes can be tied to 831.
- **New foes:** the Northumbrian levy and the men of Conaille.
- **New single-combat opponents:** Máel Brigte, Hallbjörn, Grímr Hrappsson.

## 4. Budget
About 27 scenes and 12–14k words. One run reads about 4–5k words, depending on where he sails.

## 5. Hooks into 832 (recorded as flags)
- **People:** Máel Brigte (ransomed, killed or kept), the Lindisfarne book, Kolbeinn's offer on Skye, the Danish hersir at Kaupang, Ingibjörg or Gyða, the brother feud, the Hrappsson feud.
- **Obligations:** the jarl's view of him, and the commissions kept or failed.
