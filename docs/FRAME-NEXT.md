# Frame: what comes next (diplomacy, family, great fleets, eras, new ports)

Josh's list (playtest 6):
> "I think we need to include more of these systems: Diplomacy. Family life. Joining great hordes of ships. Choosing starting date based on which era of Vikings you wish to live in (Ragnarr's days, his sons' days, etc.). More major ports, with more content around the east road and the Mediterranean, as well as Iceland."

None of this is built yet. Each needs your approval first.

## What exists to build on
- **Diplomacy:** standing in every port; seven lords and oaths; félag partners; hostages and sureties; the high king's poet.
- **Family:** a wife (four of them to court), a farm and a hall, thralls, fosterlings among the hostages. No children.
- **Great fleets:** hosts (three wings) and the joinable fleets: Dorestad 834, Rouen 841, Dublin 841, Nantes 843, Paris 845, the Liffey 837, Armagh 839–840.
- **Eras:** one start, 830, at sixteen. The legends table already runs to Harald Fairhair (885).
- **Ports:** 48 places. The east road stops at Truso. Aldeigjuborg is on the map but closed. No Mediterranean, no Iceland.

## 1. Family life, and an heir
**Problem.** A wife is a name on the Home tab. One life ends the game, so the long middle has to carry everything.

**Proposal:**
- **Children** are born to a married hero: a son or a daughter about every other winter, named by you. Some die young, as they did.
- **They grow up on the Home tab:**
  - a son can be fostered with an ally (that binds the ally; see diplomacy);
  - a son can sail with you from 14;
  - a daughter can be married for an alliance.
- **Family scenes** follow the children's ages: births, the first sword, a son's first raid, a daughter's suitor, a quarrel over the inheritance. About 20 scenes.
- **The heir.** When you die, you can go on as your eldest grown son: the same world, his father's fame half carried into his name, the hall, the hoard, the feuds and the oaths. The saga becomes a family's.

**Why first:** it answers "family life" and "eras" together: you reach Ragnarr's sons' days by living into them as your own son. It also gives the long middle a reason: the years build an heir, not only a heap.

**Size:** an engine for children and the heir; about 20 scenes, about 7,000 words.

## 2. Choosing your era
**Proposal:** at a new game, choose the era as well as the beginning:

| Era | Start | The world |
|---|---|---|
| Horik's days | 830 | As now |
| Ragnarr's days | 843 | Nantes, the Seine, Paris, Turgeis's fall, Dublin's ship-fort |
| His sons' days | 859 | Björn and Hastein's Mediterranean voyage; the Great Army gathering |
| The Great Army | 865 | Ivar, Halfdan and Ubbe in England; Alfred; York taken |
| Harald's war | 872 | Hafrsfjord; men leaving Norway for Iceland and the isles |

- **Each era needs:**
  - an opening chapter of 6–8 scenes;
  - the chronicle carried forward (it stops at 851);
  - the legends of the day in person;
  - the right places opened (York as a Norse town after 866, Iceland after 870).
- **The heir** (above) lets one life roll into the next era, so eras are also reached by playing.

**Size:** per era, about 8,000 words, chronicle entries, and a measure pass. The Great Army is the biggest.

## 3. Joining great fleets
**Problem.** The fleets you can join are single days: one battle, one sack.

**Proposal: a campaign with a host,** over one or more summers and a winter camp:
- **You choose** which leader to follow, and where your ships stand in the fleet.
- **The host moves on standing orders,** like a coast raid (`docs/FRAME-PACING.md`). Its decisions are taken at its leaders' council, where your voice counts by your ships and your name.
- **Sieges, tribute and a share-out** among captains who quarrel.
- **Winter camps:** Noirmoutier, the Seine islands, Repton and Thetford for the Great Army.
- **The flagship campaigns:** Paris 845 (extended), the Mediterranean voyage 859–861, the Great Army 865–878.

**Size:** an engine for the host on campaign (building on hosts and coast raids); about 10,000 words per flagship campaign.

## 4. Diplomacy
**Proposal:**
- **Tribute for peace.** When a coast fears you (fame, raids), its kings offer silver every year for a peace of so many summers. Breaking it costs honour along the whole coast, and the treaty can be broken against you too.
- **Marriage alliances.** Your daughter, or your sister, to a captain, a jarl or an Irish king. It binds them, and it binds you.
- **Embassies.** Send a man and a gift to a lord or a king for favour, a truce, or a hostage exchange.
- **Alliances against a rival:** two captains against a third, for a season.

**Size:** engine for treaties and embassies; about 15 scenes.

## 5. New ports
- **The east road (Austrvegr):**
  - Aldeigjuborg opened; Hólmgarðr (Novgorod); Kænugarðr (Kiev); Bolghar on the Volga;
  - Miklagarðr (Constantinople, the raid of 860), and the Varangian service later;
  - the river portages as expeditions; silver from Serkland as the trade.
- **The Mediterranean:** Seville (844), Nekor, the Balearics, the Camargue winter camp, Luna (taken for Rome, 860), Pisa. Reached with the 859 voyage, or alone by the long road.
- **Iceland:** found in the 860s (Naddoddr, Garðarr, Flóki), settled from 870. Late in life, or in Harald's war, a landnám: claim land in a new country and end your saga as a founder.

**Size:** about 15 places, about 30 scenes, about 12,000 words, with art for each region.

## Recommended order
1. **Playtest the pacing build** (built now) before adding more length.
2. **Family life and the heir:** the cheapest way to make a long life worth living, and the bridge to the eras.
3. **Ragnarr's days (843) as a second era,** with the chronicle carried to 870.
4. **The great fleet campaign,** starting with Paris 845 and the Mediterranean 859.
5. **Diplomacy:** tribute for peace and marriage alliances, which by then have children to marry.
6. **The east road, then Iceland,** then the Great Army era.
