# Vikingr: complete review, October 2026

Josh asked for "a complete review of everything we have done so far": balance, polish, depth and replayability. This review comes after ten playtests and the shore-harrying build.

**Method**
- 60 bot lives: 30 from each era, three policies, three backgrounds, 10 years each.
- The standing bot sweep: 1,500 lives.
- The duel and trade measurement tools.
- The content validator.
- The playtest notes so far.

**What is reported**
- Numbers are measured, not estimated.
- A bot is not a player. The cautions below say where that matters.

## The game as it stands

| | |
|---|---|
| Written content | 457 scenes, about 118,000 words |
| World | 51 places (35 raidable), 8 coasts, an unnamed shore along every foreign one |
| Starts | 3 backgrounds × 2 upbringings each × 2 eras (830, 843), point-buy attributes, 3 skill sets |
| Systems | The shield-wall and the duel; host battles; plunder rounds; scouting; fleets and shipmasters; trade (16 goods, moving prices); allies and the félag; lords and oaths; hostages; land, settlement and rule; family and the heir; hardship (harvest, fire, sickness, envy); great fleets; the skald; the chronicle, 830–878 |
| Engine | 56 modules, about 11,000 lines; 300 unit tests; a validator for content rules; 15 measurement tools |

## 1. Balance

### Findings

| Area | Measured | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Survival** | <ul><li>A careful raider is alive after 10 years 67–71% of the time.</li><li>A reckless one, 15–16%.</li><li>A homebody, 92%, with almost no fame.</li></ul> | Good. Risk tracks choices. |
| **Single combat** | *Corrected, see `docs/FRAME-DUEL-3.md`.* Duels cause about a quarter of deaths (33 in 120 bot lives), mostly challengers who arrive with fame before the hero has the skill to beat them. The "94–100%" figure was the hero at 35, not a young hero. The progression is sound: at 16 the hero dies to champions; at 25 he beats most. | **Real, but not the duel engine itself:** the hero accepts challenges blind, and one champion (the Wolf-coat, guile 0) is trivial to read. |
| **Raid pay** | <ul><li>A raider takes about 13 pounds a year.</li><li>A named raid averages 170–400 ørtugar.</li><li>A shore raid averages 51–87 (new this round), plus cattle and goods.</li></ul> | Good after this round's tuning. |
| **Trade** | <ul><li>A karvi trading circuit makes 6–10 pounds a summer: 50–75% of raiding, with far less risk.</li><li>A knarr is no better than a karvi at the same purse.</li></ul> | Trade's overall pay is right (the target was 60–80% of raiding). The knarr not out-earning a karvi is a small problem. |
| **Wealth over a life** | <ul><li>After 7–9 years a raider holds about 230 ørtugar (under 5 pounds) in silver and hoard.</li><li>3 lives in 30 build a hall.</li><li>In 830, 15 in 30 marry; in 843, 5 in 30.</li></ul> | **Silver doesn't build into anything.** It comes in and goes out on upkeep, shares and gear. The hall and marriage are rare outcomes, not part of the arc. 843 lives have too few marriage hooks. |
| **Hunger** | Cautious and random play spend weeks hungry (bot columns: 72 and 179). | Probably a bot artifact (bots don't buy food well). Check in a real playtest before changing anything. |
| **The Loire fights** | <ul><li>Now sized from the player's crew and fought beside allies.</li><li>The night attack on the island is held in about a quarter of lives.</li></ul> | Watch it in playtest. |

### Recommended
1. **Show the odds before a duel** (`docs/FRAME-DUEL-3.md`). The men's reckoning on every offered fight, and guile for the one trivially read champion. *(Corrected from "make the duel a contest": the engine is sound, the choice is blind.)*
2. **Give silver somewhere to build.**
   - A cheaper first step to a hall, as an extension to the farm.
   - The hall should pay back: more men at the muster, a feast multiplier already exists, and the jarl's notice.
   - Measure: target 1 in 3 raider lives with a hall by year 8.
3. **More marriage hooks in 843.** Aude is one; add two more: a Breton chief's daughter through Lambert, and a Vestfold match through Hrói's family.
4. **The knarr pays off.** Larger lots, or a market that only buys in bulk.

## 2. Polish

### Findings
- **Fixed in playtests 7–9:**
  - the calendar (season scenes mid-winter);
  - the wording of a winter abroad;
  - goods not shown;
  - fixed-size fleet fights;
  - pay below what the text said.

  No equivalent open bug is known.
- **Not yet checked in the new systems:** the harry screen has only been seen in code. It needs one human playtest on a phone.
- **243 flags are set but never read in a condition.**
  - Most are read in scene text or by the engine. The validator only checks conditions.
  - Some are genuine decoration: set to remember a choice that nothing ever recalls.
  - That breaks the project's own rule: "nothing declared is decoration".
- **3 scenes can dead-end** (the validator's warnings: `b_field`, `h_gyda`, `g_veizla`), and 6 sandbox-only placeholder scenes remain. Players never see the placeholders.
- **The full check takes about 30 minutes,** mostly copying the state on every action (43%) and plotting courses (20%). That slows every build.

### Recommended
1. **One human playtest of the shore-harrying screen and the cruise log on a phone** before building more on it.
2. **Audit the 243 unread flags.** Wire the ones that matter into a later scene or the saga; delete the rest.
3. **Fix the 3 dead-end warnings.**
4. **Speed up the engine.**
   - Copy only the parts of the state that an action changes, or skip the full copy inside the bots and the summer forecast.
   - Cache course paths per day.
   - Target: the full check under 12 minutes.

## 3. Depth

### Findings
- **Many systems, and most touch each other:**
  - fame and the skald feed the afterlife;
  - standing feeds hiring and trade;
  - the fleet feeds the hold, which feeds trade;
  - lords feed the levy, which feeds host battles.
- **The decisions a life turns on are good:**
  - service or your own ship;
  - raid or trade;
  - which lord;
  - settle abroad or not;
  - the heir.
- **Thin spots:**
  - **Men are numbers.** Only 4 sworn men are written as characters (`men.yaml`); the hird that grows later is names and loyalty. The crew has no faces after the first years.
  - **Gear is shallow.** A sword, mail and a helmet are the whole progression. The new war-gear search helps but repeats.
  - **The winter hall is a short list.** About 6 actions, the same every year.

### Recommended
1. **Characters among the crew.** A recurring cast of 6–8 oarsmen with a trait each (the joker, the Christian, the one who wants your place) who appear in banter, fights and scenes, and can die. Highest return for the writing cost.
2. **Winter projects that take more than one winter:** a new ship, a bigger hall, fostering a son, a feud settled at the thing.
3. **Gear with a story:** a named sword from a raid that its former owner's kin come to reclaim.

## 4. Replayability

### Findings
- **A life meets about 105 scenes.**
- **Two lives from the same start share about half of them:** 45% in 830, 53% in 843 (counting only what happens after the start).
- **Across 60 lives, 357 of 451 scenes were seen; 94 (21%) never were.** The largest unseen groups:
  - rule and kingship (17);
  - named places nobody sailed to (12);
  - the Seine 845 (6);
  - challengers (6);
  - lords (6);
  - expeditions (5);
  - hardship (5).

  Those are late-game, far-away or rare by design. The bots also play more narrowly than people do.
- **Two eras and three backgrounds give six real openings.** The upbringings and point-buy change the build, not the story.

### Recommended
1. **A third era**, the Great Army (865–878), as the third opening. The chronicle already runs to 878, and the legends are in the registry (Ivarr, Ubbi, Halfdan).
2. **Make the 21% reachable.** A rumour system that points a life at the rule scenes and the far places (the expeditions exist; they need a reason to go).
3. **A different first summer for each background.** In 843 all three start at the same oar; give each its own way into the fleet.

## Assumptions and risks
- **Bots play narrowly.** They never buy food well, never trade, and choose duels badly. Survival, hunger and unseen-scene counts lean pessimistic for a human; pay and fame lean about right.
- **Duel figures come from `measure:duel`** against named champions at equal skill. Real fights in a life include lesser men, which are easier.
- **The 60-life replayability sample is small.** Overlap figures are ±5 points.
