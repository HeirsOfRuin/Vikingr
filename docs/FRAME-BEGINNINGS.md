# Frame: the two other beginnings, and a slower rise

**Status:** frame for the author's approval, 2026-10-14. Nothing here is built yet.

**The author asked for two things:**
- the freedman's son and the outlaw written properly. Today both start from a Phase 1 placeholder: a borrowed karvi and four men who "did not ask";
- no more becoming "a near legend and a rich shipmaster before I am 17".

The two belong together. Every beginning should be the same kind of life: two years at another man's oar before a ship of his own, with a different road to that ship for each.

---

## Part 1: The slower rise (all three beginnings)

### What happens today
- At Bangor, if Ásgeir falls, the hero can take the Raven's steering-oar, at 16, and command her for the rest of the summer.
  - About 1 life in 6 does.
  - The author's own life took Bangor, Skye and Lindisfarne twice with her, and reached fame 47 by Yule.
- In spring 831, at 17, about a third of lives get a ship of their own: the Raven kept, the jarl's karvi, or one bought.
- A first summer's deeds count in full for an oarsman, as if they were his own victories ("Beat the Ulster levy at Bangor").
- The legends' fame in 830 is set low, because their great years come later: Ragnar 30, Turgeis 40, Horik 60. A boy of sixteen passes Ragnar by Yule.

### The target arc
| Age | Year | What he is |
|---|---|---|
| 16 | 830 | An oarsman. His own deeds are a few hard moments: the gate at Iona, the wall at Bangor. |
| 17 | 831 | Still at another man's oar, but a better one: the **prow-man** (*stafnbúi*), the place of honour and danger at the bow, with a share and a half. Captains begin to know his name. |
| 18 | 832 | **The earliest a ship of his own**, by a road that depends on his beginning. |
| 19–21 | 833–835 | The Frisian Road, as written, with his first fleet. |
| 21 or more | 835+ | Among the living legends, if he has earned it, and only then. |

### What changes
1. **Bangor (`b_oaths`) no longer gives command.**
   - **If Ásgeir falls,** Steinarr takes the steering-oar. Win or lose the challenge for it, the men won't follow a boy.
   - **Rewritten as "The prow":**
     - Steinarr gives him the prow for the rest of the summer;
     - Ketill swears to him anyway ("for later");
     - Ásgeir's sword comes to him if he carried the body.
   - He still makes the plunder choices at the abbey, but as the man at the front of the rush, not the man giving orders.
2. **The jarl's reckoning (`h_jarl`)** becomes the jarl noticing the boy who held where his sister's son fell: "Come to me when you have a beard." That is the first mark of his favour.
3. **Spring 831 (`sp_831`)** has no ship of his own.
   - **The choices are whose oar he takes:**
     - the Raven, under Steinarr, or under Ásgeir if he lived, as prow-man;
     - Harek of Orkney's ship;
     - the jarl's own ship, as one of his young hirðmen.
   - **The 831 voyages** (Kaupang, Lindisfarne, Skye, Conaille) already play at another man's oar. The few captain-only moments get oarsman versions, chiefly Ubbi at Kaupang, who now speaks to the young man rather than the captain: "when you have a ship".
4. **Spring 832 (`sp_832`)** is where the first ship comes, by the beginning's road (Parts 2 and 3); the younger son's road is the jarl's karvi or Thorkell's widow's. The yearly "a ship for sale" event waits until 832.
5. **Fame:**
   - **At another man's oar,** a battle or raid won by the crew counts at **half weight** for the oarsman. It is the captain's victory; he was there. Deeds done with his own hands count in full: the gate, the front rank, a duel, a captive king.
   - **The legends' early fame rises** so a first summer doesn't outrank them:
     - Horik 150;
     - Harald Klak 110;
     - Turgeis 70 (a rumour, growing);
     - Ragnar 55;
     - Hálfdan the Black 50.
   - **Target:**
     - at the end of 830 the median hero is outside the top five;
     - the best lives reach the top three around 834;
     - first place is possible from about 835, for the best.
6. **Measured by `measure:830`, `measure:831` and the sandbox:**
   - no hero has his own ship before spring 832;
   - 40–60% have one in 832;
   - about 75% by 833;
   - fame rank at Yule 830 as above.

## Part 2: The freedman's son (Leysingsson): "The Boatyard"

### Premise
- **Your father, Leysingr,** was born a thrall on Hrapp's farm. His mother, your grandmother **Órlaith**, was taken from the island of **Rechru** (Rathlin, off Ulster) in **795**. That was the first year the Norse ships came to Ireland; the Annals of Ulster record the burning of Rechru.
- **Hrapp freed Leysingr** when he was thirty, for twenty years of good boats. He paid for his freedom the old way, with the **freedom-ale** (*frelsisöl*): he brewed the ale and led his old master to the high seat in front of witnesses.
- **You are free, but a freedman's son:**
  - your wergild is half a free man's;
  - by the law you still owe Hrapp's family your respect at the Thing, and your father owes them work at harvest;
  - Thorgils Hrappsson has never let you forget it.

### What he has that the others don't
- **Craft and seamanship.** He caulked half the Raven's strakes.
- **Some Irish,** from his grandmother.
- **A father who can build a ship.**

### What he lacks
Standing. Bárð won't give a daughter to a freedman's son. Men laugh when he speaks at the Thing.

### Prologue (spring 830; about 3,500 words; replaces the younger son's boyhood)
1. **The Boatyard (`f_yard`).** The Raven is on the stocks for her refit, and Ásgeir comes down to see the work.
   - The boy can show what he knows (a craft check), speak up about a bad strake, or keep his head down.
   - This sets what Ásgeir thinks of him.
2. **The freedom-ale (`f_ale`, a memory of age twelve).** His father's freedom-ale at Hrapp's farm.
   - Thorgils, a boy then, pours the ale on the floor and calls it thrall-piss. The boy can fight him, swallow it, or make old Hrapp laugh at his own son.
   - This plays the part of the younger son's Hallbjörn scene and seeds the Thorgils feud early.
3. **Órlaith (`f_amma`).** The old woman by the fire, who still prays in Irish.
   - She tells of the burning of Rechru: the ships, her brothers, the boat.
   - She offers him the Irish words, a little bronze cross, and a name to say if he ever stands on an Ulster strand.
   - Choices: learn the tongue (`flag.irish`, lore up), take the cross, or refuse it all ("I'm a Northman").
4. **The bailer's place (`f_bench`).** The muster on the jarl's landing. Ásgeir takes him, but as the bailer and the ship's mender, on a half share.
   - He can take it, ask for an oar and a full share (Ásgeir: "Prove it by Orkney"), or have his father ask for him, which shames them both.
   - From here the shared muster scenes (`m_landing`, `m_farewell`) play, with lines of their own for him.

### Through the shared years (inserts in written scenes; about 3,000 words)
- **At sea:** the bailer's place gives his own lines in the storm and the calm. If he earned an oar, he moves to the benches by Orkney.
- **Iona:** with Irish, he can speak to the monk Colmán in his own tongue. That opens a different branch of `i_colman`, and a different `i_831_colman` revisit.
- **Bangor:** the captives speak Irish. He can understand what they're saying, and one of them knows Rechru.
  - Choice: free one, keep quiet, or tell Steinarr what they're planning (they mean to break for the lough).
- **Home:** his father wants to know about the Raven's strakes, not the fighting. The Hrappssons expect his father at their harvest.
- **Yule, the flyting with Thorgils:** sharper ("thrall-born"; "your grandmother's people paid for this hall"). If it comes to the holmgang, Thorgils thinks a freedman's son is cheap to kill, because the wergild is half.
- **831, Conaille:** the capture of King Máel Brigte, written for the historical year. The ship needs a man who speaks Irish at the king's door.
  - **His interpreting** is the 831 moment that makes his name: a deed of his own, while still at the oar.
  - **The cost:** these are his grandmother's people, and the scene asks what he says in Irish when the captain isn't listening.

### His road to a ship (832)
- In the winter of 831–832 he and his father **build** a karvi on the jarl's landing, at about half price, paid for with the shares of two summers.
- Hrapp's family claims a patron's due from what a freedman builds. He can:
  - pay it, which costs silver and keeps the peace;
  - refuse it at the Thing, a lawsuit, which costs standing;
  - give Thorgils the first voyage's share, which buys peace and shame together.
- **She launches in spring 832,** and nobody else in the district has a ship they built themselves.

### Marriage and later
- Bárð's price for Gyða is far higher for him (or it's refused outright until his fame passes 40).
- Ásta, the smith's widow at Kaupang, and Þórunn on Skye don't care whose son he is.
- At the 833 council the jarl asks him to look at the war fleet's keels. That's a different way into the Frisian Road.

## Part 3: The outlaw (Grimsson): "Three Winters"

### Premise
- **At sixteen,** at the spring horse-fight at the district Thing, **Ozurr Hrappsson**, the eldest of Hrapp's sons, goaded your father Grímr's stallion with a spike. Horse-fights were a notorious cause of killings, at the Thing and in the sagas. Your father struck him; Ozurr drew; you killed Ozurr with your father's axe.
- **The Thing outlaws you from Hordaland for three winters.** You may come back after that, or sooner if the wergild for Ozurr (three pounds) and the jarl's price for your peace (*landkaup*, a pound and a half) are paid.
- **Hrapp says he wants blood, not silver.**
- Your father's old shipmate Ketill tells you the Raven will put in at Birsay.

### What he has
Weapons, and a killing already in his past. Men either fear him or want to try him.

### What he lacks
A harbour. He can't go home: the jarl's men will take him, and the Hrappssons will kill him. Every winter is spent in somebody else's hall, on somebody else's terms.

### Prologue (spring 830; about 3,500 words)
1. **The horse-fight (`o_horses`).** The killing, played. How it happens is the player's choice, and it sets his honour and how hard the feud runs:
   - in hot blood;
   - after Ozurr drew first;
   - or because you meant it.
2. **The Thing (`o_thing`).** The law-speaker and the jarl. Hrapp demands full outlawry, and your father offers silver. The jarl gives three winters.
   - Choices: accept it; plead (a speech check, which could shorten it to two winters); or curse Hrapp across the mound (honour, and the feud runs hotter).
3. **The last night (`o_night`).**
   - Your mother packs your sea-chest. Your father gives you his axe and twelve coins.
   - Ketill comes to the door after dark: "The Raven sails for Orkney in a month. Ásgeir is short of men. Be at Birsay."
4. **The passage (`o_passage`).** Working his way west on an Orkney trader's knarr, with a Hrappsson cousin among the crew.
   - What happens to the cousin decides whether the feud follows him to Orkney.

### Into the shared year (inserts; about 2,500 words)
- **Birsay (`o_hall`):** the Raven comes in. Ásgeir takes him on at Ketill's word, over Steinarr's objection: "An outlaw aboard is bad luck, and worse with the jarl." From here, **Iona and Bangor play as written**, with lines of his own (the men watch the outlaw in the wall).
- **The autumn:** the Raven goes home, and he can't. He's put ashore at Birsay, or on Skye with Kolbeinn.
  - **The engine:** for an outlaw, the home fjord is barred until the three winters are served or the price is paid. He winters abroad.
- **Yule 830, abroad (`o_yule`):** in Harek's hall at Birsay. This is the outlaw's version of the Yule the younger son spends at home. In place of Thorgils's flyting:
  - Harek's daughter Ingibjörg asks him what he did;
  - the Hrappsson cousin, if he lived, starts the feud's first fight.
- **831, at Harek's oar:** Harek sails the same western summer (Skye, Lindisfarne, Conaille), so **the 831 content plays with Harek as captain**. Kaupang is outside Hordaland and open to him.
- **The hunter:** Önundr Hrappsson, the youngest and best of Hrapp's sons (already in the game as a captain and a fighter), comes west to find him in 831 or 832. It's a duel on a beach, or a deal.

### His road to a ship, and home (832–833)
- **832, a ship by the outlaw's road:**
  - Harek lends a karvi for a share;
  - he buys a hull in Dublin or Kaupang;
  - or he takes one: a prize, or a Hrappsson ship.
- **Spring 833 (`o_return`).** The three winters are served. He sails home into the 833 council (`fr_833`), and the Frisian Road begins for him there, as for the others. Two ways in:
  - **Peace:** if the wergild and the jarl's price are paid, the Hrappssons must take it before the district.
  - **Without paying:** he is home by the law, and hunted by Hrapp's sons.
- **If he never comes back,** the Danes take him: Ubbi's road to Dorestad doesn't need Hordaland.

## Part 4: The engine
- **Background inserts.** `background == freedman` and `background == outlaw` in scene conditions and text. Both exist already.
- **Outlawry.** While `flag.outlawed` is set and the time isn't served, the home fjord can't be sailed to. Winter happens abroad. Spring starts at sea with a background scene (`o_spring_831`, `o_spring_832`) in place of the muster.
- **The prow-man.** In service, a shares bonus (1.5) and the front rank as his place. It's a reward from scenes, not a rank system.
- **Building with his father.** A scene effect: a karvi at half price, launched in spring.
- **The fame rules:**
  - oarsman's battle and raid deeds at half weight;
  - new legend curves in `rivals.yaml`.
- **Removed:** the Phase 1 placeholder starts and the borrowed karvi for these two beginnings.

## Decisions for the author (recommended in brackets)
1. **The earliest ship:** [spring 832, age 18, for everyone.]
   - The alternative is 831, at 17, only for a life that won the challenge for the Raven at Bangor.
2. **The outlaw's victim:** [Ozurr Hrappsson.] This ties the outlaw into the Hrappsson feud that already runs through the younger son's game, and makes Önundr his hunter. The alternative is a new family.
3. **The outlaw's way home:** [three winters by the law, with or without the wergild.] Paying the wergild buys peace with the Hrappssons; not paying brings him home hunted.
4. **The freedman's grandmother taken at Rechru in 795,** the first recorded raid on Ireland: [yes.] It puts his family's story at the very beginning of the age.
5. **Ásgeir's fall at Bangor:** [it stays about as common as now (four lives in ten), but it no longer gives the hero the ship.]
