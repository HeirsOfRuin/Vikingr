# Frame: combat, second form

Josh, 2026-10-15: "I'm thinking we need to overhaul the whole combat situation. It doesn't feel like there is any real strategy or tactics, just guessing and getting lucky." He agreed to my recommendation:
- **Battles:** deployment before the fight, then rounds in which the enemy's next move is shown, both lines have stamina, and every order shows its projected outcome before you commit.
- **Duels:** a stance, distance and stamina fight with the opponent's move shown.

This is a **preview**. Three fights use the new systems: the shield-wall at Bangor, the Yule holmgang with Thorgils, and the fight on the beach at Lindisfarne (831). Every other fight keeps the old system until Josh gives a verdict.

Bangor is Ásgeir's wall, so deployment is not yours there. To try deployment, use the chapter jump "Lindisfarne, 831, as captain": as captain, you choose where your sworn men stand, how many men are held back and where you stand. Lindisfarne is an easy fight against a monastery's levy: instinct 99%, always push 75%, random orders 62% (300 runs).

## The problem with the old system
- **Hidden rolls decided fights.** The enemy's order was a weighted draw you could only lean against, and a counter table you learned by losing.
- **Nothing carried over between rounds.** No stamina, no ground, no spent spears, so no reason to hold something back or set up a later round.
- **Orders were guesses.** The stopgap (each order saying what it beats) helped, but you still guessed what they would do.

## The design rule
**You see what they are about to do, and what each answer will cost, before you commit. Chance only moves the numbers a little (±15%); it never decides what beats what.** The skill is in spending limited breath, spears, shields and reserve at the right moment. It is not in guessing.

## Battles: the shield-wall

### Deployment (before round 1)
Three questions, one at a time, when it is his own wall. In another man's wall (Bangor, as Ásgeir's oarsman) none of them is his: the scene has already asked where he stands, and the fight starts at round 1.

| Question | Options | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Sworn men | Front rank / spread through the line / back with the reserve | +15% blows; or cohesion +1; or safe until the reserve goes in, then cohesion +1 |
| Reserve | None / a few (15%) / strong (30%) | Fewer men in the wall now; fresh men and breath later, and the answer to a flank |
| Your place | Front / in the wall / behind it | Front: +10% blows, cohesion +0.5, most danger. Behind: you see their move after next. |

### Each round
- **Shown:** their move this round. From behind the wall you also see the one after it.
- **Your order**, from six, each with its projection on the button:

| Order | Breath | Does | Beats | Loses to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lock shields | +1 | Little harm either way | A charge (their wedge breaks), a volley | A flank |
| Push | −2 | Even trade; gains ground if you are clearly stronger | A reforming or throwing line | Locked shields, a feigned retreat |
| Throw spears (2 a battle) | −1 | Harm for little loss | A push or charge coming on, a reforming line | Gives ground to a push |
| Charge | −3 | Heavy harm both ways | A tired line, a volley | Locked shields, a feigned retreat |
| Fall back a step | +3 | Gives ground, recovers breath | A charge, a flank | Gives up ground (3 back and you break) |
| Throw in the reserve | +3 | Fresh men and breath, once | A flank | |

- **The enemy's moves:** lock shields, push, volley, charge, feigned retreat, flank, reform.
- **The enemy learns.** Push every round and he locks shields and feigns; stand still every round and he flanks.
- **A line breaks** when its cohesion reaches 0 or it is pushed back 3 steps. After 8 rounds both sides part.
- **Breath matters.** A line's blows scale with its breath (from 40% when blown to 100% when fresh). Under 3 breath you cannot charge.

### Worked example: Bangor, the Irish warned (40 Raven men, 30 Irish)
| Round | They show | Projections that matter | Order | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Charge (next: volley) | Lock shields: they lose ~3, you ~2, their line 7.0 → 4.2. Charge: they lose ~9, you ~8, their line → 2.9 | Charge | They lose 10, you 7. Their line 2.5. |
| 2 | Volley (next: feint) | Charge: closes before the second volley, their line → 1.2 | Charge | They lose 2, you 1. Your breath down to 2. |
| 3 | Feigned retreat | Push: "they give, you follow, and they turn on you", your line 5.0 → 3.5 | Lock shields | Nothing lost. Breath back to 3. |
| 4 | Volley | Charge: their line 1.1 → 0.0 | Charge | They break and run. |

The safer line in round 1 was **Lock shields** (their wedge breaks for a loss of 2). It costs a round but keeps your breath. Either answer works. Pushing into the feigned retreat in round 3 is the trap.

### Measured (400 runs each, Bangor)
Where he stands is chosen in the scene before the fight: at Ásgeir's shoulder (front rank, +10% to the line) or in the wall.

| Play | Warned, front rank | Warned, in the wall | Surprised, front rank | Surprised, in the wall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Instinct ("let the men handle it") | 79% won | 67% | 97% | 91% |
| Charge whenever you can, else random | 49% | 36% | 81% | 75% |
| Always push | 32% | 18% | 62% | 46% |
| Random orders | 35% | 23% | 55% | 47% |
| Always lock shields | 3% | 1% | 13% | 10% |

Before this pass, always push won 75–82%: it won by ground alone in three rounds. Two changes fixed that:
- **Feints give no ground.** A push into a feigned retreat gains nothing.
- **Pushing lines only gain ground when clearly stronger.** You need 25% more men and breath together.

## Duels: single combat

### Each exchange
- **Shown:** his next move. With wits and cunning together at 6 or more, you also see the move after it.
- **Your answer**, from five, each with its projection: damage both ways, breath, shields and steps from the edge.

| Move | Breath | Beats | Loses to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Great blow | −3 | A quick cut (you take the cut, he takes far more), a bare or late shield | A shield rush (you are knocked off your line), a raised shield (it splits) |
| Quick cut | −1 | A shield rush (he goes past, off balance), a man stepping back | A great blow, a raised shield |
| Shield up | +1 | A great blow (the board splits, you take nothing), a quick cut | A shield rush (off balance) |
| Shield rush | −2 | A great blow (he is off his line), a raised shield, a man stepping back (2 steps) | A quick cut |
| Step back | +3 | Gets breath | Costs ground: three steps and you are off the cloak and have run |

- **Off balance.** A man knocked off his line takes 1.5× and deals 0.5× next exchange, and his shield comes up late against a great blow. Rush, then the great blow, is the combination to look for.
- **The bigger man.** Rushing a stronger man's great blow still knocks him off his line, but you take half the blow.
- **Tired.** At 2 breath or less your blows land at 60%.
- **The holmgang cloak.** Three shields each. Three steps back is off the cloak. He may yield when he has bled to half his strength.
- **Instinct is not thought.** "Fight on instinct" plays the right answer most of the time, but a man untrained in weapons falls back on a great blow about one exchange in three (less with skill). Reading him yourself beats it.

### Worked example: the holmgang, the younger son (14 hp) against Thorgils (16 hp, swings first and keeps swinging)
| Exchange | He shows | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Great blow | Shield rush | Knocks him off his line for 2 breath. Shield up would cost a board. |
| 2 | Shield up (off balance) | Great blow | His shield comes up late: about 4 through it, and his board cracks. |
| 3 | Great blow | Step back | Your breath is 3. Let it cut air and get 3 back, one step toward the edge. |
| 4 | Shield rush | Quick cut | He goes past you off balance, cut. |
| 5 | Shield up (off balance) | Great blow | Through the late shield again. Blood on the cloak: he may yield. |

Ketill's advice in the scene ("take it on the rim and let him tire") is a good line too, but it costs shields.

### Measured (400 runs each, against Thorgils)
| Play | Younger son: won / dead | Freedman's son: won / dead |
|---|---|---|
| Instinct (never yielding when offered) | 82% / 10% | 71% / 14% |
| Random | 39% / 25% | 22% / 31% |
| Always great blow | 33% / 36% | 27% / 37% |
| Always shield up | 1% / 16% | 1% / 14% |
| Always quick cut | 0% / 54% | 0% / 51% |

**Careful reading** (the best answer each exchange) wins about 95% and never dies. Thorgils is meant to be readable; Ketill tells you how he fights.

## What stays the same
- Both systems sit inside the old battle and duel state. Wounds, the dead, sworn men falling, deeds, fame and the story's win and lose links are unchanged.
- The hero's chance of being cut down in the wall uses the same per-place risk as now, scaled by how hard the round was.

## Open questions for Josh's verdict
1. **Does it feel like tactics now?** Do you read their move and answer it, or still guess?
2. **Is there too much on the buttons?** Each shows losses, both lines' cohesion, breath and ground.
3. **Length.** Bangor runs 4–7 rounds and a holmgang 6–12 exchanges. Is that right, or too slow for a fight that recurs every summer?
4. **Readable foes.** All tells are honest now. Should cunning foes sometimes show a false tell (a feint habit), with a warning that he is hard to read?

## After the verdict
1. Convert every battle and duel to the new systems, including hosts (three wings, each its own wall) and boarding fights (no falling back on a ship).
2. Retune the bots and the measurements against the new fights.
3. Rebalance hardship against healthy crews (deferred from the morale fix).
