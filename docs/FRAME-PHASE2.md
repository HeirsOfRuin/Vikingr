**The year in one line:** a sixteen-year-old with nothing goes west on another man's ship. He comes home with a name and the chance of a ship of his own, or he does not come home.

---

## 1. The big decision: does he start with a ship?

The Phase 1 placeholder gives him a borrowed karvi and four men at 16. That works against the core of the game, which is building from nothing. The rank ladder (§8) starts at oarsman for a reason.

**Recommendation: he starts as an oarsman on Ásgeir Kolsson's ship and can win command during the year.**

- **Spring and summer: he rows.**
  - The captain chooses the course, and the hero can argue for a different one at the ship's council.
  - His own choices are personal-scale: where he sits at the oar, where he stands in the wall, whether he goes over the wall first, what he takes, who he spares.
  - The map shows the voyage as it happens.
- **Bangor is the turning point.** Ásgeir may fall, or be badly wounded, or lose the crew's trust. There are several routes to command:
  - the crew acclaims him for his deeds;
  - Ketill backs him;
  - he challenges Ásgeir's second;
  - Ásgeir names him if he lives and was impressed;
  - none of these, and he serves on.
- **The voyage home** is his to steer if he took command, using the Phase 1 sailing systems.
- **The four sworn men** (Ketill, Ulf, Hallvard, Bersi) begin as his bench-mates. They swear to him only when they choose to. That oath is the first real thing he earns.
- **Spring 831 opens on what he has:** his own ship (one of several routes), a share in one, or a bench in another man's ship.

This needs one engine addition: **service**, a mode in which the captain decides the course. Sailing then happens through scenes, using the same voyage resolution as free sailing.

The alternative is to keep the borrowed karvi from day one. It is simpler to build, but it skips the first rung of the ladder.

## 2. The beats

Each major beat has 3–5 approaches, as in Adalia. Pool events sit between beats so two runs of the same year differ. Rough scene counts are shown in brackets.

### 0. Boyhood: the stories (4 short scenes, ages 8–15)
Each scene sets a lean (a starting skill or trait) and a relationship.
1. **The ship that came home** (age 8). A ship comes up the fjord low in the water. What does the boy watch?
   - the men unloading: Seamanship;
   - the swords: Weapons;
   - the old men's stories: Lore and Speech;
   - the silver on the scales: Trade.
2. **Hallbjörn** (age 12). A fight with the elder brother who will have the farm. Approaches: fight him, outwit him, take the beating, tell father. Each sets the brother's attitude, from rival to grudging ally.
3. **Ormr One-Hand** (age 14). The old raider at the hearth tells it differently every winter. One night he tells it straight, and gives the boy something: a seax, a rune-stick, or a piece of advice that turns out to matter. This is the first hint of the gods, and it is ambiguous.
4. **Father's word** (winter, age 15). The farm goes to Hallbjörn. Father has bought him a place at Ásgeir's oars. How he leaves:
   - with a blessing;
   - with his grandfather's sword taken without asking;
   - with a promise to Gyða, the girl at the next farm;
   - with nothing said.

### 1. The muster (March–April 830) [3]
- **Ásgeir's ship** at the jarl's landing, and the four men he will row beside.
- **His place at the oar:** the prow benches, where the prow men fight first and are seen, or the safe middle of the ship.
- **First impressions:** Ulf's mouth, Bersi's arithmetic, Hallvard's ravens, Ketill's silence, and the fact that Ketill rowed with his father.
- **Approaches to standing out:**
  - lift the heavier chest;
  - out-row a man twice his age;
  - fix the steering oar;
  - keep his head down.

### 2. The crossing and the islands (May) [pool of about 8; a run sees 3–4]
- Seasickness in front of everyone (comedy).
- A whale under the hull.
- Wreckage and a drowned man.
- A storm.
- A strange sail.
- Rowing in a dead calm, with Ulf's songs.
- Hallvard and the ravens.
- Bersi's dice.

Banter on every crossing.

### 3. The hall at Birsay, Orkney [4]
- The chieftain's hall. Men who have been south tell you where the silver is.
- **The ship's council:** Iona first or Bangor first, and he can argue (a Speech check).
- **A drinking contest** with an Orkneyman (comedy, with small stakes and a big story).
- **Kálfr the skald** is looking for a crew worth making verses about. He can come aboard, now or later.
- **Approaches:** listen, boast, pick a fight, court the chieftain's daughter (a light hook), slip out.

### 4. Iona [6–8]
The first raid, using the full raid sequence (§11.3).
- **Approach:**
  - land at dawn in the bay;
  - come over the hill at night;
  - pose as traders or pilgrims, which needs someone with Irish;
  - or go straight in.
- **Assault:** a small shield-wall against lay brothers and tenants. The first-over-the-wall moment can come here.
- **Plunder against the alarm.** Shrines, plate, books with gilded covers (which the men want to burn for the gold), cattle and captives.
- **What he does with people:** kill, drive off, spare, or take captives for the slave markets. Each choice has consequences, in line with §1a: never glorified, never his act where the content lines say so.
- **Aftermath:** a monk who speaks a little Norse (Brother Colmán) may become a hook. The burning, and whether he puts it out.
- **Hallvard and the monks' god:** an omen that cuts both ways.

### 5. Bangor [6–8]
The real fight, and the gate to command.
- **Approach:** the lough, the levy close by, the abbey's walls.
- **The shield-wall against the Ulster levy:** javelins, feigned retreats, and the hero's position chosen.
- **Ásgeir's fate**, which varies with what happened (a target of about 40–60% of runs where he dies or is badly wounded): killed, maimed, or alive and ashamed or alive and impressed.
- **The command gate:** acclamation, Ketill's backing, a challenge, Ásgeir's word, or none.
- **The way home:** in command, the hero sails it himself, with storm risk in September. Otherwise it is a scene.

### 6. Home and the share-out (October) [3–4]
- **The shares:** dividing them if he commands, receiving his own if he does not. The jarl's cut either way.
- **Hallbjörn and the farm:** pride, envy, or a debt.
- **Gyða:** whether she waited, depending on the promise.
- **Captives:** sold, kept as thralls on the farm, or ransomed back, each with consequences.

### 7. Yule: the feast, the flyting and the völva [5–6]
- **The bragarfull, the boast cup:** the vows made over it become obligations (boasts), to keep or break in 831.
- **Flyting with Thorgils Hrappsson,** a contest of insults in front of the hall. Win and the hall laughs with you; lose and it laughs at you. Either way it can escalate.
- **Heiðr the völva** sees for the hall. Her prophecies are drawn from what will really come, through the omen system: one about the hero, one about a sworn man, one about the century.
- **A nickname is given.** The hall names him for what he did: glorious, comic or cruel. He does not choose it.

### 8. The holmgang [2–3]
- **How it can come about:** from the flyting, a boast, or a slight to a sworn man.
- **Alternatives to fighting:** pay wergild at the Thing, laugh it off, or have Ketill talk him out of it.
- **The holmgang itself:** fought by holmgang rules, with the yield on offer.

### 9. Spring 831 [1–2]
- **A chapter card:** where he stands, what he has, who is with him, what he owes.
- **The gate result:**
  - his own ship: Ásgeir's, the jarl's old karvi on loan, a share bought, or a ship taken;
  - or another man's bench.
- **The hooks into 831.**

## 4. Systems to build (engine additions)

1. **Service:** the hero rows in another man's ship, and the course is set by scenes (a `voyage: { to }` effect using the existing day-by-day voyage). Free sailing opens when he takes command.
2. **The full raid:** approach, assault (existing shield-wall), plunder rounds against an alarm clock, captives as a choice, aftermath. This replaces the one surprise roll in `startRaid`.
3. **Flyting:** a word duel built on the duel exchange structure. The moves are boast, insult, deflect, laugh it off and turn the hall; wit and presence matter. The result is the hall's laughter, which feeds fame or shame.
4. **Boasts:** vows at the bragarfull become queued obligations with deadlines.
5. **Nicknames:** rules that read deeds, wounds, comic events and flyting results. One is guaranteed by Yule.
6. **Crew banter:** short lines keyed by temperament, recent events and the man's mood. Shown on crossings and after fights.
7. **Omens and prophecy:** pre-rolled future events made into omens that are right about 70% of the time (§12).
8. **Wounds on the sworn men,** and a death that is mourned.
9. **Continue pages:** `[break]` inside scene text, so long scenes are read in beats.
10. **Chapter cards,** from Adalia's `cards.ts`.
11. **Readable playthroughs:** scripted plans and transcripts, ported from Adalia (`tools/plans/`, `tools/transcript.ts`).
12. **Scene art:** an `art:` key on a scene, the scene renderer, and the feast and shield-wall animations (DESIGN §19b).
