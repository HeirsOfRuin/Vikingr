Vikingr

A life in the Viking age, 830 to 878. From an oar-bench in another man's ship to a hall, ships and land of your own.

You start with almost nothing, rowing in another captain's ship. What follows is up to you: the monasteries of Iona and Bangor, the trading beaches of Kaupang and Hedeby, the Irish ship-forts, the Frankish rivers, and in time the Great Army in England.

It is a story, read in short scenes with choices, and a strategy game underneath. You sign on men and keep them fed and paid. You buy and mend ships. You fight in the shield-wall and in single combat, plunder and trade, swear to a lord or refuse to, take land and try to hold it through the winters you are away. Your fame grows when your deeds are told at a feast. When you die, your son can go on with what is left.

The world follows the annals: the Irish, Frankish and English chronicles, and the sagas written down later. Real men such as Turgeis, Ragnarr and Ívarr come and go on their own dates.

Play the demo free

The demo is the first two years, 830 and 831, in your browser. When it ends, the game gives you a save code: load it into the full game and your life goes on from the same spring.

The full game

  • 646 written scenes, about 69,000 words of story, with over 1,500 choices.
  • 56 places to sail to, from Iceland to Garðaríki and from Hordaland to Aquitaine.
  • 3 starting eras: Horik's days (830), Ragnarr's days (843) and the Great Army (865).
  • 3 backgrounds (younger son, outlaw, freedman) and 6 upbringings, each with its own boyhood.
  • 13 historical rivals whose fame rises against yours, and lords to serve, defy or betray.
  • Shield-wall battles and single combat, round by round. Plunder, trade, captives and ransom.
  • Land of your own: farms bought abroad, ship-forts taken and garrisoned, lordships, and in the end a sea-jarldom.
  • A family and an heir: when you die, a son can carry the saga on.

About it

  • Made for a phone, and plays on a computer too. The full game is a download: one file that opens in any browser and plays offline (not Safari: it will not keep saves from a file), and an Android app.
  • Your life is saved on your own device. The menu copies it out as a short text save code, to move it between devices or keep it safe.
  • No account, no ads, no analytics. Nothing leaves the page.
  • The 9th century was brutal. Killing, slavery and death are part of this game. They are never glorified.

Made with AI assistance. I designed the game, its systems and its world, and directed, edited and play-tested it; the code, the text and the pictures were generated with AI models to that design, and the pictures redrawn in the game's own fixed palette.
